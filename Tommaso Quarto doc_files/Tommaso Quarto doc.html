Description of variables

variables of Interest

  • Adult smoking (in percentage of total of population of adults): This variable describes the reported rate of current daily smokers in the population of the county.

  • Excessive drinking: This variable describes the self-reported rate of excessive drinkers of the population in the county.

  • Teen births: This variable describes the amount of teen births per 1000 females aged 15-19.

Main variable

  • Education level (percent of 25 > year olds that have a Bachelors degree minimum): This variable describes the percentage of 25 year olds or older that have a diploma for a Bachelors degree or higher.

Control variables to complete model:

  • Unemployment: This variable describes the unemployment rate in the county. Unemployed people are 16 years old or older and are actively looking for work.

  • Income Inequality: This variable describes the income inequality levels, computed as the ratio of income at the 80th percentile over the income at the 20th percentile.

  • Severe Housing Problems: this variable describes the percentage of households that have at least one of the 4 reported housing problems: high housing costs, overcrowding, lack of kitchen or plumbing facilities.

  • Children in single parent households: This variable describes the percentage of children that grow up in single parent households currently.

  • Mental health providers: This variable describes the rate of mental health providers per 100000 inhabitants.

Description of the data joining and cleaning

The raw data for health was at first downloaded from the CHR&R website for all the years we are interested in for this study (2020-2023) as 4 different csv files (one for each year). After downloading the data in Rstudio using vroom(), the the raw columns names were replaced with the true column names which were situated in the first row. Furthermore, all the columns which were not useful were removed in order to keep only the variables used in this study, namely: % Severe Housing Problems, Income Ratio, % Unemployed, Mental Health Provider Rate, Teen Birth Rate, % Excessive Drinking, % Smokers, % Children in Single-Parent Households. Moreover, the missing values for county names were replaced by “Average” as for each county there was one observation per year calculating its average in the raw dataset. The nature of the variables was also changed: county and state are characters, and the rest are numerical. Finally, after having cleaned the 4 years datasets, they were merged together with the column (state, county), ensuring every state and county are the correct key. After the merge the dataset was pivoted to be in long format (with each observation a unique combination of State, county and year), and the columns were renamed as needed.

Description of the sources

The Health dataset used in this study is constructed of data coming entirely from the CHR&R (County Health Rankings and Roadmaps) program. This initiative was created by the University of Wisconsin Population Health institute with support from the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation. It consists in providing annual data on health factors within and across almost the entirety of counties from the United States. The CHR&R provides data, evidence, guidance and examples to build awareness about the multiple factors influencing health conditions and disparities across the country. Furthermore, CHR&R puts an emphasis on the range of health-influencing measures that offer crucial context about community specific health conditions. In fact, Along with the data the program provides tools and evidence-based strategies, which can be used by the communities to move to action and solve the important health and societal issues concerning their people. As an example, The “2025 Annual data release” includes over 80 health-related measures, and focuses on the impact of certain societal factors present in the United States on community conditions.
